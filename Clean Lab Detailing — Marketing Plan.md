# Clean Lab Detailing — Marketing Plan

Oct 29, 2026 · @James

The goal for the next six months: build proof (reviews, photos) and repeat customers through winter, so Clean Lab opens spring 2027 with a booked calendar instead of starting from zero.

## Where things stand

The site at [cleanlabdetailing.com](https://www.cleanlabdetailing.com/) is a strong base. It has clear packages from $50, 11 city landing pages for local search, an owner story, and an FAQ. What it lacks is the proof and the hooks that turn a visitor into a booked, repeat customer.

**Working well**

- Clear three-tier pricing with add-ons, and a "we bring everything" message.
- Service-area pages for Sheffield Lake, Avon Lake, Lorain, Avon, Elyria, Westlake and 5 more.
- Evening and weekend positioning, which suits busy households.
- A real face and direct phone line, which builds trust for a one-person business.

**Gaps**

- No customer reviews or testimonials on the page, while it claims a 5-star rating and 100+ cars.
- No before/after photos, the single most persuasive asset in detailing.
- No way to buy a gift card, join a maintenance plan, or refer a friend.
- Booking is a request form with a 24-hour confirmation, not a calendar with real slots.
- Nothing addresses winter, when outdoor spigots in Ohio get shut off and exterior demand drops.

## Positioning and pricing

Clean Lab is priced well under nearby mobile detailers, which helps win first jobs but caps what each limited evening and weekend slot earns. Position it as the precise, owner-run detailer for Lake Erie shoreline suburbs, and raise prices on a schedule as reviews come in.

**Positioning line:** "Lab-precise detailing in your driveway — owner-done, evenings and weekends, Sheffield Lake to Westlake."

**Pricing moves**

1. Add vehicle-size tiers now: sedan / SUV / truck-van. A 3-row SUV takes far longer than a coupe at the same $120.
2. Label current prices as "launch pricing" on the site. Step up roughly 15–20% once you pass 25 Google reviews (target: March 2027).
3. Lead with the Full Detail in all marketing; the $50 wash is an entry point, not the headline.
4. Take a small deposit ($20–25) at booking to cut no-shows, which hurt more when slots are scarce.

Open question: are the "100+ cars" and "5★" stats on the homepage current counts? If not yet, swap them for real numbers — a visitor who searches and finds few reviews will distrust the rest.

## Channels, in priority order

For a one-person mobile business, the best channels are cheap, local and compounding: your Google profile, the neighbors of every car you do, and repeat customers. Paid ads come after those are working.

### 1. Google Business Profile and reviews

This is the highest-return item. Most local detailing searches end in the map pack, and reviews decide who gets the call.

- Set up as a service-area business (hide your home address), listing all 11 towns.
- Primary category "Car detailing service"; add "Mobile car detailing" if it's offered.
- Post 3–5 before/after photos a week and one Google post weekly (offer, tip or recent job).
- Ask for a review at every walkthrough: hand over a small card with a QR code, then text the link the same evening.
- Reply to every review within 24 hours, using the town name naturally.
- Also claim Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp and Nextdoor business pages with identical name, phone and hours.

Target: 10 reviews by Jan 1, 25 by March 31.

### 2. Driveway and neighborhood marketing

Every job is a billboard on a street of likely customers. Use it.

- Put a branded yard sign or A-frame at the end of the driveway while you work.
- After each job, leave door hangers on 10–15 nearby houses: "I just detailed a car on your street — $15 off if you book this week."
- Offer a "street day": 3+ neighbors book the same day and each gets $10 off. Less driving, more cars per evening.
- Post in Sheffield Lake, Avon Lake and Lorain Facebook community groups and on Nextdoor with before/after photos (follow each group's business-post rules).

### 3. Short-form video

Before/after reveals are some of the best-performing local service content. One phone, a tripod, 30 seconds per job.

- Formats: dirty-to-clean reveal, "worst interior of the week," pet hair removal, salt stains in winter, one-tip-in-60-seconds.
- Post the same clip to Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts and the Google profile.
- Always say the town in the caption ("Avon Lake SUV interior") for local reach.
- Cadence: 3 posts a week, batched from the weekend's jobs.

### 4. Google Ads (keep it tight)

Run the existing campaign lean until reviews and photos are in place, then scale what books.

- Geo-target only the 11 service towns; call extension on; schedule ads around when you can answer.
- Bid on intent phrases: "mobile detailing near me," "car detailing Avon Lake," "interior detailing Lorain."
- Negative keywords: car wash, jobs, hiring, supplies, DIY, how to, free.
- Winter: shift budget to interior-detail and gift-card ads.

### 5. Partnerships

One good partner can send a steady stream of jobs.

- Realtors and lenders: closing gift cards ("Full Detail for your new driveway"), sold in packs of 5.
- Independent used-car lots and small dealers: retail-ready interiors on evenings or weekends.
- Local businesses: a 10% employee perk, or a "detail day" at an office lot when your schedule allows.

### 6. Referrals and repeat business

A repeat customer costs nothing to acquire. Build this in from day one.

- Referral offer: give $15, get $15, tracked by a code or the customer's name at booking.
- Maintenance plan: a monthly or every-other-week exterior wash at a set price, with first pick of time slots. Recurring revenue fills your calendar first.
- Rebooking texts: a reminder 6–8 weeks after each detail with a one-tap booking link.
- Keep a simple customer list (name, car, town, last service, how they found you) — it becomes your email and text list.

## Seasonal plan: November to April

Winter changes the offer, not the effort. Most customers shut off outdoor spigots by late November and nobody wants an exterior wash at 25°F, so sell interiors, gift cards and spring pre-bookings until April.

**Winter service menu**

- Interior Detail: needs only an outlet, so it works all winter.
- Garage details: full service for customers with a heated or attached garage.
- Rinseless exterior wash: bring your own warm water on days above freezing; good for salt and grime.
- "Winter Interior Reset": salt stains, floor mats, slush-soaked carpets.

| Month | Theme | Key moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November | Foundation + holiday launch | Website fixes; Google profile complete; gift cards live by Nov 15; "ready for holiday guests" interior promo; start review asks |
| December | Gift cards | Push gift cards on social, Google posts and ads ("the gift every car owner wants"); realtor closing-gift packs; last-minute digital cards through Dec 24 |
| January | Winter Interior Reset | Salt-stain and mat promo; video of salt removal; rebooking texts to fall customers; reach out to 3 used-car lots |
| February | Pre-sell spring | Launch the "Spring Reset" early-bird: book and pay a deposit in Feb–Mar for an April slot at a discount; launch the maintenance plan waitlist |
| March | Ramp up | Raise prices if 25 reviews reached; reactivate Google Ads on exterior terms; print door hangers and yard sign; open maintenance plans |
| April | Spring rush | Work the pre-booked calendar; door hangers after every job; street-day offers; ask every customer for a review and referral |

## Lean budget

The plan runs on about $250–400 a month, with most of the impact coming from free work (reviews, photos, door-to-door, posting). Figures are estimates in USD.

| Item | Cost | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Ads | $150–300 / month | Low in Dec–Feb, scale from March |
| Door hangers (500) + review QR cards | \~$60–100 one-time | Order in March; small batch now |
| Yard sign or A-frame | \~$40–80 one-time | Before spring |
| Booking software with deposits | $0–30 / month | November |
| Boosted Facebook/Nextdoor posts | $0–50 / month | Gift card push in December |
| Google profile, social, referrals | Free | Ongoing |

Rule of thumb: keep paid spend under 10–15% of detailing revenue, and cut any channel that can't show a booked job within 60 days.
